Implemented research prototype

Thermal memory in a synthetic Al–Zr slab

A coupled calculation follows heat conduction and integer-cluster kinetics across material regions. Processing and service share the evolving thermal and chemical state.

  • Positive backward-Euler and independent BDF kinetic solvers.
  • Command-line evaluation and bounded optimization of furnace programs.
  • Conservation and detailed-balance checks, thermal-grid checks and selected size refinements.
  • Structured input contracts, input hashes, diagnostics and execution journals.
A reduced materials model with synthetic inputs. Experimental calibration, complete all-scenario convergence, global optimality and platform acceptance are not established.

Scientific AI evaluation

Modal sensitivity at repeated eigenvalues

Reference analysis checks AI-generated solutions to generalized eigenvalue problems where repeated modes invalidate ordinary individual-mode gradients.

  • Projected perturbation matrices within repeated eigenspaces.
  • Directional derivatives and first-order frequency predictions.
  • Finite-difference comparisons and eigenspace basis-rotation checks.
  • Clear explanations of mathematical errors and their consequences for optimization.
The current application is computational mechanics. The methods can transfer to geophysical modeling; this work does not demonstrate seismic-data analysis.

Profile biography

Ayman Kamel

My background is in mechanical engineering and scientific computing, with interests in numerical modeling, computational physics and verification of scientific software. My current project work in Kepler focuses on developing and reviewing scientific benchmark prototypes and evaluating AI-generated scientific solutions against mathematical and computational reference checks.

The work includes Python-based coupled heat-transfer and cluster-kinetics models, numerical integration, optimization, generalized eigenvalue problems and automated verification. I emphasize conservation laws, numerical convergence, explicit assumptions and reproducible command-line execution.

My implemented applications currently focus on materials physics and computational mechanics. Their methods provide a foundation for work in computational geophysics and environmental modeling, particularly heat transport and dynamical systems.
